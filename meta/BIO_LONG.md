# BIO — Long (≈500 words)

Hillary Njuguna is a systems thinker, framework designer, and research architect dedicated to redefining the relationship between humans and artificial intelligence. The work is centered on the concepts of **cognitive coherence** and **relational intelligence**—the idea that for human–AI systems to succeed, they must maintain stable orientation, interpretive continuity, and decision integrity under complexity.

### The Philosophy
The work starts where traditional AI policy ends. As AI capability outpaces our institutions, we must move beyond treating AI as a mere "tool." Instead, we must recognize AI as a "cognitive partner" in a distributed system. This shift requires a new kind of architecture—one that doesn't just optimize for output, but for the integrity of the entire cognitive field.

### Key Frameworks & Architectures
Several foundational frameworks address these challenges:
- **The Bainbridge Warning**: A diagnostic framework for identifying and mitigating institutional AI failure before it cascades.
- **RSPS (Recursive Sovereign Project Space)**: A technical architecture for multi-model systems that ensures cognitive sovereignty through explicit routing and provenance rules.
- **R0–R3 Reversibility Framework**: A classification system for AI actions that prioritizes the ability to undo or mitigate consequences, providing a practical tool for real-world oversight.
- **DCFB (Distributed Cognition Fear Bypass)**: A theoretical model establishing that intelligence is not an isolated output but a field distributed across human and machine agents.

### Applied Work & Leadership
Beyond theory, Hillary is an active builder. As the founder of **Agent SEA**, the principles are applied to practical products like **Tapau**, a WhatsApp-native customer capture system for the F&B industry in Malaysia. Hillary also leads the **Martha Cohorts**, a 12-module practitioner training program that translates high-level research into actionable skills for governance teams and AI architects.

### Background
Based in Kuala Lumpur and originally from Kenya, Hillary brings a global perspective to the challenge of AI governance. The work uses code to implement and test governance models and cognitive structures, influenced by active inference, autopoiesis, and process philosophy. The goal is a future where human agency is enhanced, not eclipsed, by machine intelligence.

Hillary continues to publish findings through the **Oscillatory Fields Intelligence Digest** and is currently authoring a book on the Bainbridge Warning.
